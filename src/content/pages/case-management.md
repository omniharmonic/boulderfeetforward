When someone is ready to go further than a meal and a conversation, our peers become their case managers. This is the long game: sustained, individualized support, advocacy, coordination and follow-through toward goals the person has set for themselves.

There's no waiting list and no program to fit into. We ask what this person needs to move forward, and then we help make it happen.

Case management can look like:

- **Housing assessments and Coordinated Entry**, done on site, starting from where you are.
- **IDs and vital documents**, including paying the small fees that stand in the way.
- **Benefits enrollment and advocacy**, like SNAP and Medicaid, Aid to the Needy Disabled, SSI and SSDI.
- **Health and behavioral health care**, with warm handoffs: we introduce you to the provider, help you through intake, get you to the appointment, and follow up afterward.
- **Employment, education and legal resources.**
- **Problem-solving when something falls through.** If something doesn't work, we troubleshoot, try something else, and learn from it.

We don't duplicate the larger system of care. We extend its reach, working with existing providers and care teams so people can get services they might otherwise never reach.

## What outcomes look like

Housing matters, and we also know there is more than one way home. In a typical year, about 20 people who've worked with us reach stable housing. For about 5 of them, our part is limited to basic needs. We count the connection, but we don't claim the housing as ours. Another 10 are people we support as part of a larger care team. For about 5, we're the primary support helping them get there.

Of the housing outcomes where we're the primary support, about four out of five are self-resolutions: individualized paths home that the person identified and pursued themselves.

## How to start

Come to either weekly event and ask for a peer, or call or email us. Friday Breakfast Club is followed by drop-in hours for case management, troubleshooting, or just talking as peers.
